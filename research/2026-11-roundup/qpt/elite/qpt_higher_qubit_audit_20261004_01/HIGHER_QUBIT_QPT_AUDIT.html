Higher-qubit QPT evidence audit

What survives, what must be relabeled, and what must be rerun
Date: 4 October 2026
Repository: stochastic-frames at d62fcf192f27d8fb207ce61a1c466fc5059a7e1f
Decision: the previous higher-qubit work is not worthless. It remains valid evidence for a physically meaningful local-product-input, local-Pauli QPT benchmark. What does not survive is its attribution as a reproduction of Quiroga’s reduced global-probe experiment, or the transfer of its tuned constants and performance claims to that different sensing geometry.
Main consequence. The old engineering and scaling work is retained. The old empirical curves are retained and relabeled. Any headline claim about FRAMES versus Quiroga under Quiroga-style sensing must use the corrected campaign or be rerun. No historical artifact should be deleted.

1. Scope and audit method

This audit reviewed the active repository, Git history, documented sensing operators, tests, all named local QPT result trees, four shareable QPT PDFs, the verified September snapshot, and a bounded read-only inventory of the recorded Ruche result tree. No optimization was run and no historical artifact was modified or deleted.

  • The active repository was clean and synchronized with origin/stochastic-frames.
  • The legacy numerical implementation was checked against commit 423990f; its core structured-data, operator, generator and GPU-runner files are unchanged at current HEAD.
  • The Quiroga backend was added separately in fe68eb4, hardened in 74c968a, and given a reproducible campaign runner in d62fcf1.
  • Classification means: RETAIN as-is; RELABEL without discarding; RERUN before making the target claim; EXCLUDE from the primary comparison while preserving the record.

2. The two experiments are genuinely different

Feature Previous higher-qubit benchmark Current Quiroga-style benchmark
Input states 4n product states from {|0⟩, |1⟩, |+⟩, |+i⟩}⊗n d2=4n global states: basis states plus all pairwise real and imaginary superpositions
Measurement 3n local X/Y/Z settings with 2n outcomes One generalized 2d-outcome POVM
Rows 24n; 331,776 at n=4 and 110,075,314,176 at n=8 2d3=2·8n; 8,192 at n=4 and 33,554,432 at n=8
Coordinates Normalized [I, X, −iY, Z] tensor basis Choi/matrix-unit coordinates
Loss normalization Half mean squared residual Quiroga’s printed rule uses half sum squared residual
Typical data audited Fixed row-indexed additive Gaussian noise, commonly σ=0.05 Current n=2/n=4 campaign is noiseless
Physical scope Conventional full QPT with product preparation and local readout Reduced low-rank/near-unitary design; probes are already partly entangled at n=2

The objective-scale difference matters directly. The corrected campaign maps the mean-loss FRAMES convention to a fixed Quiroga TP weight using β=m/(2λ). Old β, λ, step sizes, batch sizes and schedules therefore cannot be transferred numerically.

The geometry also changed. At n=4, the measured informative RMS scale of the reduced POVM is about 0.0023, versus a local-Pauli probability standard deviation around 0.058. The reduced design is much less well conditioned for this optimizer. Its failure is not explained by “fewer rows” alone.

Physical meaning

The old sensing matrix is not an arbitrary or unphysical random matrix. It comes from valid product input states and local Pauli outcome projectors. Its results remain physically interpretable as a simulated local-Pauli QPT protocol. The Gaussian observation model is synthetic and can yield values outside [0,1], so it is not literal finite-shot data.

The new design is mathematically physical—the POVM is positive and complete— but the repository models its probabilities rather than a device circuit. Preparing its global probes and implementing the generalized POVM would require nonlocal operations and possibly an ancilla dilation. Its 2d-outcome POVM is informationally complete for pure states, not generally for arbitrary high-rank mixed outputs.

Wording discipline: the current n=4 campaign is a documented Quiroga-style higher-dimensional extension, not an exact published n=4 reproduction. Quiroga does not provide that experiment, the POVM constants, the adaptive-step proportionality constant, or the initialization normalization.

4. What the old “recovery” results mean under the current gate

The present noiseless gate requires fidelity ≥0.99, RMSE ≤10−6 and normalized TP ≤10−3, where normalized TP is ||T(UU†)−I||F/√d. The old noisy campaigns were not designed around this gate and generally did not record its RMSE. Applying it retrospectively is therefore a diagnostic, not a fair restatement of their original goal.

Legacy highlighted endpoint Fidelity Normalized TP Current-gate interpretation

n=5 selected local-Pauli run 0.906799 0.01369 Fails fidelity and TP
n=8 original “99%” endpoint 0.990027 0.01244 Real 99% factor overlap; TP is about 12.4× above the gate

Interpretation: no earlier higher-qubit endpoint is a documented pass of the present three-part gate. This does not erase the old n=8 result: it remains a verified high-factor-fidelity result on a noisy local-Pauli instance. It should be called that, rather than “exact recovery” or “Quiroga-style recovery.”

For comparison, the corrected n=4 held-out campaign also does not currently establish FRAMES recovery: FRAMES fidelity is 0.0129–0.0778 with normalized TP 0.00347–0.00354 at 2,000 effective row epochs. The printed adaFGD update passes TP but has fidelity 0.000789–0.01164. Exploratory 20,000-step Armijo FGD recovers all four seeds, demonstrating that the corrected inverse problem is solvable.

Manuscript impact

The repository contains no manuscript TeX or bibliography, so the actual Section 5.2 and manuscript-integrated figures could not be edited or checked. The required correction is already documented in docs/qpt_quiroga_sensing.md, lines 285–320.

Recommended manuscript wording.
“We evaluate two distinct QPT sensing designs. Benchmark A uses product inputs and local Pauli measurements, giving 24n probability rows; its measurement loss is mean-normalized. Benchmark B follows the reduced global-probe/2d-POVM construction of Quiroga and Kyrillidis, giving 2·8n rows. The two designs have different signal scale and conditioning, so optimizer constants and row budgets are not transferred between them. Higher-qubit results from Benchmark A do not reproduce the experiments of Quiroga and Kyrillidis.”

7. Decision for the project

  1. Keep the old high-qubit work. Present it as a separate local-Pauli/QPT_BFW benchmark and retain its engineering contributions.
  2. Correct attribution before reuse. Do not call the old 24n design Quiroga’s setup or say it faithfully reproduces that paper.
  3. Do not claim a previous strict-gate pass. The older runs optimized noisy factor fidelity and approximate TP; they lack the present noiseless RMSE certificate.
  4. Use the d62fcf1 n=2/n=4 campaign as the primary current comparison. State that n=4 is a principled extension, not a published reproduction.
  5. Rerun only claims that need the global design. Momentum, MiFGD, higher-qubit FRAMES, noise robustness and sample-efficiency claims require new experiments if they are to appear in the primary Quiroga-style comparison.
  6. Do not jump directly to an expensive n=8 conclusion. First determine at n=4 whether extended-budget FRAMES can converge and satisfy TP on the corrected geometry. That separates an iteration-budget problem from a structural fixed-penalty problem.
Final answer: the earlier higher-qubit program survives as a substantial alternative physical benchmark and engineering result. What is currently unvalidated is the transfer of its performance story to Quiroga-style global sensing.

8. Limitations and evidence references

  • No manuscript source was available in the repository.
  • Old campaigns generally did not save the RMSE required by the new gate.
  • Several highlighted old results use one channel/seed.
  • The old Gaussian-noise and current noiseless campaigns answer different statistical questions.
  • The current global-probe backend has no fused GPU runner, so legacy GPU scaling cannot be transferred.
  • General higher-rank recovery is not certified by the 2d-outcome POVM.
