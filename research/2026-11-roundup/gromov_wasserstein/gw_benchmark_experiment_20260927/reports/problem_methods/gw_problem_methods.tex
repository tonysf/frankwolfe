{\color{ink}\fontsize{16}{19}\selectfont\bfseries GW matching: the composite formulation\par}
{\small Mathematical formulation of the two methods used in the experiment\par}
\vspace{3pt}

Let $A\in\R^{n\times n}$ and $B\in\R^{m\times m}$ be symmetric distance matrices for two views of a dataset. Let $a\in\R^n_{++}$ and $b\in\R^m_{++}$ sum to one; our experiments use uniform weights. The variable $P\in\R^{n\times m}$ is a transport coupling. Define
\begin{align*}
 f(P)&=\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\sum_{j,\ell=1}^{m}(A_{ik}-B_{j\ell})^2P_{ij}P_{k\ell},\\
 C&=\{P\geq0:P\one_m=a\},
 &T(P)&=P^\top\one_n,\\
 D&=\{b\},
 &g(y)&=\iota_D(y)=\begin{cases}0,&y=b,\\+\infty,&y\ne b.\end{cases}
\end{align*}
Thus $C$ imposes nonnegativity and row masses; $g\circ T$ imposes column masses. The benchmark is exactly
\[
 \boxed{\min_{P\in C}\; f(P)+g(TP)}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \min_{P\in U(a,b)}f(P),\qquad
 U(a,b):=\{P\geq0:P\one_m=a,\ P^\top\one_n=b\}.
\]
Here $f$ is smooth and generally nonconvex, $g$ is a convex indicator, $C$ is compact and convex, and $T$ is linear. There is no entropy regularization in this objective. Matrix inner products are Frobenius inner products, so
\[
 T^*u=\one_nu^\top,\qquad
 \ip{P}{T^*u}=\langle TP,u\rangle,\qquad \|T\|=\sqrt n.
\]

\textbf{Stochastic FraMES: smooth $g$, and keep the simple constraint $C$.}
For $\mu>0$, the proximal map and Moreau envelope of $g$ are explicit:
\[
 \operatorname{prox}_{\mu g}(y)=b,\qquad
 g_\mu(y)=\min_z\left\{g(z)+\frac{\|z-y\|_2^2}{2\mu}\right\}
         =\frac{\|y-b\|_2^2}{2\mu}.
\]
FraMES therefore takes Frank--Wolfe steps for the changing smooth surrogate
\[
 F_\mu(P)=f(P)+\frac{\|P^\top\one_n-b\|_2^2}{2\mu},\qquad
 \nabla(g_\mu\circ T)(P)=\frac{\one_n(P^\top\one_n-b)^\top}{\mu}.
\]
Let $\widehat G_k$ be a fresh unbiased estimate of $\nabla f(P_k)$. Momentum is applied to this estimate, while the column-penalty gradient is evaluated at the current iterate:
\begin{align*}
 d_0&=\widehat G_0,\qquad
 d_k=(1-\rho_k)d_{k-1}+\rho_k\widehat G_k\quad(k\geq1),\\
 H_k&=d_k+\mu_k^{-1}T^*(TP_k-b),\\
 S_k&\in\argmin_{S\in C}\ip{H_k}{S},\qquad
 P_{k+1}=(1-\gamma_k)P_k+\gamma_kS_k.
\end{align*}
The linear minimization oracle separates by row: choose
$j_i\in\argmin_j(H_k)_{ij}$, set $(S_k)_{i j_i}=a_i$, and set the other entries to zero. Each step preserves $P_k\in C$; column masses may differ from $b$ until repaired or approached through decreasing smoothing. The implemented schedules are
\[
 \gamma_k=\gamma_0(k+1)^{-3/4},\qquad
 \rho_k=\rho_0(k+1)^{-1/2},\qquad
 \mu_k=\frac{s_\mu}{m}(k+1)^{-1/4}.
\]
{\small To match the manuscript's $T=\mathrm{Id}$ notation, set
$\widetilde D=\{P:P^\top\one_n=b\}$. Then
$\operatorname{dist}_F(P,\widetilde D)^2=\|TP-b\|_2^2/n$;
its smoothing parameter $\beta=\mu/n$ gives precisely the same penalty. The gradient of the penalty above has Lipschitz constant $n/\mu$.}

\textbf{Stochastic entropic mirror descent: enforce $C$ and $TP=b$ in each proximal step.}
Absorb the composite constraint into $X=U(a,b)=C\cap T^{-1}(D)$. In the notation of Fatkhullin--He (2024), take the smooth expected objective to be $f$, the convex regularizer to be zero, and the distance-generating function to be negative entropy:
\[
 \omega(P)=\sum_{ij}P_{ij}(\log P_{ij}-1),\qquad
 D_\omega(Q,P)=\KL(Q\|P)
 =\sum_{ij}\left[Q_{ij}\log\frac{Q_{ij}}{P_{ij}}-Q_{ij}+P_{ij}\right].
\]
With a positive initial coupling and a fresh gradient estimate $\widehat G_k$, its ideal update is
\begin{align*}
 P_{k+1}
 &=\argmin_{Q\in C}\left\{\ip{\widehat G_k}{Q}+g(TQ)
                       +\eta_k^{-1}\KL(Q\|P_k)\right\}\\
 &=\boxed{\argmin_{Q\in U(a,b)}
          \left\{\ip{\widehat G_k}{Q}+\eta_k^{-1}\KL(Q\|P_k)\right\}.}
\end{align*}
Thus $g\circ T$ remains an exact constraint in the mathematical subproblem. There is no Moreau smoothing or momentum in this method. Its entropy term defines the update geometry; the objective being minimized remains the same $f$.

The KL subproblem is solved by matrix scaling. With componentwise operations, let
\[
 K_k=P_k\odot\exp(-\eta_k\widehat G_k),\qquad
 P_{k+1}=\operatorname{diag}(u)K_k\operatorname{diag}(v).
\]
Sinkhorn alternates $u\leftarrow a/(K_kv)$ and $v\leftarrow b/(K_k^\top u)$ to enforce both marginals. This is a \emph{KL/Bregman projection} of $K_k$ onto $U(a,b)$, rather than a Euclidean projection. Our implementation uses stable log-domain arithmetic and accepts a numerical step only when both the summed marginal $\ell_1$ residual and normalized guarded primal--dual gap are at most $10^{-7}$.

For $C_{\max}=\max_{i,k,j,\ell}(A_{ik}-B_{j\ell})^2>0$, the implemented admissible family is
\[
 \eta_k=\frac{c}{4C_{\max}\sqrt{k+1}},\qquad 0<c\leq1.
\]
Indeed, on $U(a,b)$ the quadratic remainder is bounded by
$C_{\max}\|Q-P\|_1^2\leq2C_{\max}\KL(Q\|P)$, yielding a relative-smoothness constant $2C_{\max}$. The paper's update assumes an exact proximal minimizer; our finite inner tolerance is a numerical choice, with no inexact outer theorem claimed.

\textbf{The common stochastic oracle.}
Both methods sample the gradient of the full, fixed GW objective. For
$r=P\one_m$, $c_P=P^\top\one_n$, and entrywise squares $A^{\odot2},B^{\odot2}$,
\[
 \nabla f(P)=2(A^{\odot2}r)\one_m^\top
          +2\one_n(B^{\odot2}c_P)^\top-4APB.
\]
The current marginals must be used here: FraMES iterates need not satisfy $c_P=b$.
For fresh $Z_k\in\{\!-1,1\}^{m\times q}$ with independent entries and $q=128$, the implementation uses
\[
 \widehat G_k=2(A^{\odot2}r)\one_m^\top
 +2\one_n(B^{\odot2}c_P)^\top
 -\frac{2}{q}\left[(AP_kBZ_k)Z_k^\top+(AP_kZ_k)(BZ_k)^\top\right].
\]
Since $\mathbb E[Z_kZ_k^\top/q]=I_m$, this is conditionally unbiased:
$\mathbb E[\widehat G_k\mid P_k]=\nabla f(P_k)$. FraMES averages these estimates through $d_k$; SMD uses $\widehat G_k$ directly. Both use one oracle call per update; FraMES reuses the first call for $d_0$.

\textbf{The computational distinction.}
FraMES handles the column constraint through its explicit quadratic penalty and uses only a rowwise linear minimization oracle. SMD solves a KL transport projection imposing both marginals each update. Reported endpoint losses evaluate the same $f$ after a common transport-repair procedure. Those empirical losses are separate from the methods' respective stationarity guarantees and SMD's prescribed randomly selected output.
